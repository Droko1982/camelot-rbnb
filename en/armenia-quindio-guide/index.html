Guide to Armenia and Quindío

Everything you can do from Camelot: from downtown Armenia to the towns and valleys of the Coffee Cultural Landscape.

Armenia, the Miracle City

Armenia is the capital of Quindío and one of the gateways to Colombia’s Coffee Cultural Landscape, declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2011. It sits about 1,480 meters (≈ 4,860 ft) above sea level and enjoys a mild climate year-round.

It’s known as the Miracle City for the way it rebuilt itself after the 1999 earthquake. Today it’s a friendly, green and well-connected city, from which you can reach almost every attraction in the department in under an hour.

Quindío Guide

Getting here

By plane

El Edén International Airport (AXM) is about 25 minutes from downtown by taxi, with daily flights from Bogotá and other cities. Pereira’s airport (PEI) is about an hour away.

By bus

Armenia’s Terminal de Transportes (bus station) receives buses from Bogotá, Medellín, Cali and Pereira, and frequent buses leave from there for Salento, Filandia, Montenegro and Calarcá.

By car

Armenia is about 3.5 hours from Cali, around 6 from Medellín and 6 to 8 from Bogotá, depending on traffic and weather. Pereira is about an hour away.

Things to do near Camelot

Approximate times from downtown Armenia. Check opening hours and prices with official sources before you go.

01

Plaza de Bolívar & Cathedral

≈ 8 min on foot · 0.7 km

The heart of Armenia: the triangular cathedral, the Monumento al Esfuerzo and the bustle of downtown, just a few blocks from Camelot.

Stroll through at sunset and continue along Calle Real (Carrera 14) to Parque Sucre.

OpenStreetMap
02

Parque de la Vida

≈ 10 min by car · 3 km

The city’s great green lung: lakes, waterfalls, bridges and trails winding through bamboo groves.

Perfect for a morning walk or jog.

OpenStreetMap
03

Museo del Oro Quimbaya

≈ 12 min by car · 5 km

The Quimbaya Gold Museum showcases the goldwork and pottery of the Quimbaya people in a brick building designed by architect Rogelio Salmona.

Check the official opening hours before you go; admission is usually free.

OpenStreetMap
04

Jardín Botánico & Butterfly House

≈ 25 min by car · 10 km

In Calarcá: Andean forest, suspension bridges and a butterfly house shaped like a butterfly.

Go early: the butterflies are most active in the morning sun.

OpenStreetMap
05

Parque del Café

≈ 30 min by car · 13 km

Colombia’s National Coffee Park, a theme park in Montenegro with rides, coffee culture and live shows.

It opens seasonally and on weekends; check the official calendar.

OpenStreetMap
06

PANACA

≈ 45 min by car · 27 km

An agricultural theme park in Quimbaya where you can get to know Colombian farm life, its animals and traditions.

A perfect outing with kids; set aside a full day.

OpenStreetMap
07

Salento & Cocora Valley

≈ 55 min by car · 35 km

Quindío’s most colorful town and the valley of the wax palm, Colombia’s national tree.

Willys jeeps to Cocora leave from Salento’s main square. Bring a jacket and boots.

OpenStreetMap
08

Filandia

≈ 50 min by car · 32 km

Streets lined with bahareque (wattle-and-daub) houses, handwoven vine crafts and a lookout tower with views over three departments.

Stay for sunset and enjoy a coffee around the main square.

OpenStreetMap

Your week in the realm

A suggested itinerary for guests staying seven nights or more.

  1. Day 1

    Downtown

    Plaza de Bolívar, the Cathedral, Calle Real and a traditional lunch.

  2. Day 2

    Parque del Café

    A full day of coffee culture and rides in Montenegro.

  3. Day 3

    Salento & Cocora

    A Willys jeep ride to the valley, a hike among the wax palms and trout in Salento.

  4. Day 4

    Filandia

    Crafts, the lookout tower and sunset in one of the prettiest towns in Colombia.

  5. Day 5

    Botanical garden & coffee farm

    The butterfly house in Calarcá and a farm tour to follow coffee from seed to cup.

  6. Day 6

    PANACA

    The Colombian countryside up close, ideal if you’re traveling with kids.

  7. Day 7

    Rest day

    Parque de la Vida, Museo del Oro Quimbaya and a good cup of coffee to wrap up the week.

What to try

  • Specialty coffee

    You’re in coffee country: order a pour-over and ask about the origin of the beans.

  • Trout

    A Salento specialty, served al ajillo (with garlic) or grilled, with crispy patacón (fried green plantain).

  • Bandeja paisa and beans

    For hiking days: beans, rice, chicharrón (pork crackling), arepa and avocado.

  • Tropical fruits

    Lulo, passion fruit, granadilla, soursop: as fresh juice or straight from the market.

Practical tips

  • Weather

    Mild, with sunny mornings and afternoon showers. The rainiest seasons are usually April–May and October–November: bring a light jacket and an umbrella.

  • Money

    There are banks and ATMs just a few blocks away downtown. Carry cash for the towns, the Willys jeeps and small shops.

  • Getting around

    Taxis are inexpensive; order one through an app or ask at the front desk. For the towns, buses from the terminal are frequent and safe.

  • Emergencies

    Colombia’s single emergency number is 123. If you need a doctor, we’ll point you to the nearest clinic.
